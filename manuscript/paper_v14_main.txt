Abstract
Background Over the years, researchers have noticed that a prior Epstein-Barr virus (EBV) infection is
present in almost all multiple sclerosis patients, with EBV seroconversion being followed by an increase in
serological neurofilament light chain, a biomarker indicating neuroaxonal injury. Molecular mimicry between
viral and self-antigens is one proposed mechanism for this association.
Results This hypothesis-generation pipeline incorporated NetMHCIIpan, MixMHC2pred, nominally TCR-
exposed BLOSUM62 scoring, and structural stress tests to route EBV–human peptide pairs to experimental
peptide-HLA binding and register mapping. Across 6,400 allele-specific comparisons in four HLA class II con-
texts, BALF5627-641-TALDO1108-122 (HLA-DRB1*13:03; rank 13/1,600) and BALF5627-641-TALDO1216-230
(HLA-DRB1*15:01; rank 14/1,600) were sequence-prioritized. In a separate enriched 49-pair evaluation panel,
these were the only two pairs meeting the locked strict three-output, dual-arm presentation-support/register
routing criterion at the permissive 20% percentile cutoff; neither met it at 10% or below. The constrained se-
quence null did not yield a BH-adjusted q < 0.05 for any audited pair, and both leads had within-allele maxT
p = 1.0. Thus, routing status is not statistical evidence for a sequence-mimicry signal. Structural models were
sensitive to peptide input length and template choice, and exact-allele direct-binding evidence was identified
only for the DRB1*15:01 viral arm.
Conclusions The study results provide a prioritized shortlist for experimental binding, register-mapping,
and T-cell cross-reactivity testing.
1 Background
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is an autoimmune disease that can cause degeneration of motor, sensory, visual, and
cognitive abilities through immune-mediated damage affecting myelin sheaths in the brain, spinal cord, and optic
nerves [2]. In 2022, Bjornevik et al. studied a cohort of more than 10 million U.S. military personnel, 955
of whom developed MS. Their analysis reported an approximately 32-fold increase in MS risk following EBV
infection; cytomegalovirus showed no comparable association. Serum neurofilament light chain increased only
after EBV infection, indicating that EBV may precede detectable neurological injury [1]. Lanz et al. identified
an antibody from an MS patient that recognized both EBV EBNA1 and the human CNS protein GlialCAM [3].
Previous studies such as Lanz et al. identified cross-reactive antibodies [3]; comprehensive mapping of T-
cell cross-reactivity remains incompletely defined. T-cell receptors (TCRs) recognize a peptide presented in the
binding groove of a human leukocyte antigen (HLA) molecule rather than the peptide alone. Sequence-only
peptide comparisons therefore do not define the HLA-binding component of immune recognition. Different
HLA alleles also show distinct structural preferences, affecting pMHC binding affinity and the binding register-
the placement of the nine-amino-acid core within the HLA groove. The register determines which residues are
anchors and which are potential TCR contacts [21, 22].
1
The computational pipeline developed here integrates peptide-HLA binding predictions, register-aware se-
quence alignments, and predicted peptide-HLA structures to prioritize a small subset of viral-self peptide pairs
before laboratory testing. Predicted sequence or structural resemblance does not establish stable cell-surface
presentation, functional T-cell activation in vivo, or experimentally confirmed registers.
2 Materials and Methods
2.1 Study design
This study describes a computational prioritization pipeline designed to route EBV–human self-peptide pairs to
subsequent experimental peptide-HLA binding and binding-register validation. The workflow integrated pre-
dicted HLA-II presentation support, register-aware sequence alignments, structural modeling, and downstream
evidence review. Computational predictions alone do not establish natural processing and presentation in vivo,
surface pMHC stability, TCR recognition, functional cross-reactivity, molecular mimicry, or MS relevance.
2.2 Peptide curation and allele-specific pairing
An EBV repertoire was curated from the Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) by filtering EBV source peptides
with positive T-cell assay outcomes from MHC class II alleles in human hosts [4]. Human self-proteins included
myelin-associated and CNS proteins from positive IEDB MHC-II T-cell records and additional 15-mer peptides
derived from UniProt sequences [5]. Duplicate assay records sharing accession, coordinates, and sequence were
merged. Imported IEDB records lacking an “Exact Epitope” classification or outside 11–30 amino acids were
removed, as were sequences containing X, B, or Z. The immutable curation files retain the source record, exclusion
flag, duplicate-resolution key, protein-coverage rule, peptide length, and final membership for every arm. Forty
EBV and forty human peptides were retained per allele and paired within each allele to create 6,400 comparisons.
This curated, disease-informed input universe is not a proteome-wide sample and cannot estimate a population
frequency of mimicry-like pairs.
The initial sequence screen was limited to HLA-DRB1*03:01, HLA-DRB1*08:01, HLA-DRB1*13:03, and
HLA-DRB1*15:01. Each HLA allele was evaluated independently; pMHCs from different alleles were not com-
pared. HLA-DRA*01:01 was the alpha chain in every pMHC model.
2.3 TCR-facing sequence-similarity scoring
The original 6,400-comparison V3 matrix contains one frozen, declared nine-residue core for each peptide arm.
These declared cores were used as the input to the initial score; they were not changed after pairs were ranked. The
local V2 register records identify the core caller as the IEDB recommended-binding service and retain the input
peptide, allele, returned core_peptide, rank, IC50, and raw-response filename. The service’s internal model
version, invocation date, and any service-side tie behavior were not captured in the frozen response and cannot
be reconstructed from the local files. The V3 protocol lock fixes the downstream ordering as TCR-facing BLO-
SUM62 score (descending), physicochemical mismatch (ascending), TCR-facing identity (descending), local-
surface percentile (ascending), and lexical pair identifier (ascending). The initial ranking is therefore conditional
on this declared-core input, while later predictor calls were used for audit and sensitivity analyses rather than to
retrospectively replace a V3 core.
Sequence similarity was calculated with BLOSUM62 [8]. For cores a and b, at I= {P2, P3, P5, P7, P8}, the
normalized score was S(a, b) = ∑i∈I B(ai, bi) / ∑i∈I max{B(ai, ai), B(bi, bi)}. Thus, the denominator is the
sum of the larger residue-specific self-substitution score at each compared position. Scores were calculated only
2
for the nominally TCR-exposed/core non-anchor positions P2, P3, P5, P7, and P8 in aligned nine-residue binding
cores. This custom normalization is bounded above by 1 for exact identity across the compared positions; lower
values reflect less favorable BLOSUM62 substitution support. Higher scores indicate greater substitution-matrix
resemblance at this nominally exposed interface; they are not measures of T-cell recognition, cross-reactivity,
presentation, or molecular mimicry. Percentile rankings were calculated independently within each allele’s 1,600-
pair cohort.
The two later lexicographic tie-break quantities were fixed, model-derived descriptors rather than additional
biological evidence layers. Physicochemical mismatch summarizes scaled charge, hydropathy, hydrogen-bond
donor/acceptor, and aromaticity differences at the nominally exposed residues. Local-surface percentile is the
within-allele mean percentile of six peptide-surface features, each conservatively summarized by its 75th per-
centile across available model combinations: side-chain exposure mismatch, exposure-weighted centroid and
orientation differences, exposed-residue distance-matrix difference, chemistry mismatch, and exposed-backbone
RMSD. It is used only after the sequence keys and has no value when a structure is incomplete or fails the declared
fixed-coordinate register-sensitivity rule (Supplementary Methods SM1).
2.4 Evidence audit and candidate prioritization
Eight candidate pairs selected from the 6,400-comparison matrix underwent a standardized manual evidence
audit. Predicted binding support, predicted register agreement, IEDB sequence and provenance records, public
immunopeptidome evidence, viral sequence conservation, and human self-protein tissue expression or rarity were
evaluated. Pairs were classified as high priority, medium priority, or hold. No pair was high priority; two were
medium priority; and six were held because one or both peptide arms did not meet binding criteria, provenance
was unverified, immunopeptidome support was absent, or registers disagreed. The medium-priority pairs were
BALF5627-641-TALDO1108-122 in HLA-DRB1*13:03 and BALF5627-641-TALDO1216-230 in HLA-DRB1*15:01.
The eight-pair audit categories were separate from the later 49-pair provisional classifications.
2.5 Binding predictions and binding-register analysis
For the initial eight-pair review, peptide-HLA class II percentile ranks were predicted with NetMHCIIpan-4.3
EL and MixMHC2pred context predictions [6, 7]. These are presentation/eluted-ligand-oriented computational
outputs, not direct equilibrium-affinity measurements. The terms “presentation-support” and “routing criterion”
are used throughout for these outputs; they are not called a binding gate. Full computational support required
both outputs to return ranks of ≤ 5% for both viral and self peptide arms. Lesser support required at least one
output at ≤ 5% or both outputs at ≤ 20%. Direct binding is described only when an exact peptide–allele assay
record was identified.
Binding-register analysis defined the register as the predicted P1-P9 core within the open-ended HLA class
II groove [21]. NetMHCIIpan-4.3 EL and MixMHC2pred context cores were compared. Any different core
sequence, including shifted or overlapping nine-residue windows, was recorded as register disagreement. Major
anchor positions (P1, P4, P6, P9) were separated from predicted TCR-facing positions (P2, P3, P5, P7, P8) for
downstream analysis.
2.6 Expanded 49-pair cohort selection
The expanded 49-pair cohort was analyzed separately from the initial eight-pair evidence audit. All final pair
identifiers matched entries in the original 6,400-comparison V3 matrix. The final cohort contained seven HLA-
DRB1*03:01 pairs, 14 HLA-DRB1*08:01 pairs, 16 HLA-DRB1*13:03 pairs, and 12 HLA-DRB1*15:01 pairs,
3
corresponding to 98 peptide arms. Thus, the expansion increased candidate coverage while retaining the same
four alleles represented in the initial eight-pair audit (Supplementary Methods SM2).
2.7 Secondary provisional-tier rules
Secondary provisional tiers were assigned after the expanded 49-pair screen as routing labels for subsequent ex-
perimental binding and register-mapping work. No candidate received high-priority status because the automated
public-evidence endpoint was unavailable under the frozen review protocol. These provisional labels do not estab-
lish peptide-HLA affinity, natural presentation, T-cell recognition, cross-reactivity, molecular mimicry, or disease
relevance (Supplementary Methods SM2).
2.8 pMHC structural modeling
Heterotrimeric pMHC complexes (HLA-DRA, relevant HLA-DR beta chain, and target peptide) were modeled
using the AlphaFold 3 web server, with five returned models per job [10]. Quality checks verified coordinate
completion; peptide pLDDT and peptide-chain ipTM were reported separately because well-defined HLA chains
can dominate global complex confidence. The first 85 Cα atoms of HLA chain A and chain B were aligned
by Kabsch fitting. Peptide backbone RMSD was then measured over an 11-mer window nested around the pro-
posed nine-residue core. AlphaFold outputs were treated as predicted models, not experimental coordinates, and
analyzed independently of TCR-docking simulations.
2.9 PANDORA/MODELLER register stress test
PANDORA coupled with MODELLER was used as a cross-method, register-sensitivity stress test rather
than as a binding-affinity or TCR-recognition model [17, 18]. Two preselected, forced pMHC-II templates
were used: 1BX2 (primary) and 6CQQ (sensitivity template). For each template, only the first pMHC
copy was retained and remapped to chains M/N/P; TCR chains and duplicate pMHC copies were excluded.
Targets used HLA-DRA*01:01 and HLA-DRB1*15:01 sequences. Target termini were retained, rather
than silently removed by template alignment. Each template–peptide–register condition requested 20 slow-
loop-refinement models, with up to four local jobs. The local runtime was pinned to PANDORA commit
618bbb573645bd428697666475a2e47a2e418d01; the locked environment record includes the MOD-
ELLER/PANDORA runtime probe. Reciprocal 1BX2/6CQQ calibration required complete 20-model runs,
no target-template leakage, one expected-register top-five model below 2 Å, and a lower top-five median
expected-register RMSD than both adjacent register decoys (Supplementary Methods SM8).
2.10 Calibration and exploratory modeling
The literature-derived Hy.2E11 cross-reactive benchmark, BALF5627-641-MBP85-99, served as a structural-
comparison reference [11]. The benchmark contains HLA-DRA*01:01-HLA-DRB5*01:01 for BALF5 (PDB:
1H15) and HLA-DRA*01:01-HLA-DRB1*15:01 for MBP (PDB: 1BX2). It was therefore treated as a DR15
haplotype-level control, rather than a same-allele comparison; it does not validate either BALF5-TALDO1 pair
or establish biological mimicry.
Poisson-Boltzmann/APBS pMHC surface-potential comparison was explored as an exploratory surface-
resemblance descriptor [12]. Its score was excluded from primary ranking because a 30-result control gate
yielded 15 failed and 12 missing results. Candidate outputs were excluded from ranking and cannot support
shared-TCR or cross-reactivity claims; BALF5 replicate models remain exploratory internal predictions rather
than docking validation (Supplementary Methods SM8).

2.11 Statistical calibration and sensitivity analyses
Statistical methods were implemented to test whether observed TCR-facing BLOSUM62 scores exceeded scores
expected after disruption of residue-position alignments. Each HLA allele had its own separate simulation with a
starting universe of 40 EBV cores and 40 self cores, making 1,600 pairwise comparisons. Ten thousand simulation
replicates were completed independently for each allele at random seed 20260914 (Supplementary Methods
SM3).
Next, threshold sensitivity of the frozen 49-pair panel was evaluated using percentile ranks from the IEDB-
recommended binding method, NetMHCIIpan 4.3 EL, and MixMHC2pred 2.1 context. The specific percentile
thresholds were 1%, 2%, 5%, 10%, and the explicitly permissive 20%, with exact-core and ±1-residue register
rules evaluated separately (Supplementary Methods SM3). The three outputs are correlated computational inputs,
not independent biological measurements.
Four-metric rank sensitivity was also performed on the four allele-specific cohorts containing 1,600 pairs
each. Pairs were evaluated using nominally TCR-exposed BLOSUM62, full-core BLOSUM62, nominally TCR-
exposed sequence identity, and Grantham distance. A candidate was termed multi-metric rank-concordant when
it was within the top 1% under at least three metrics; it was partially rank-concordant when it did not meet that
criterion but was within the top 5% under at least three metrics. This is a heuristic robustness check, not empirical
validation that the score predicts TCR cross-recognition (Supplementary Methods SM3).
2.12 Reproducibility
The local reproducibility package retains source files, software environments, random seeds, input logs, response-
file references, and SHA-256 dataset checksums, including the complete 6,400-pair universe and the 49-pair
continuous predictor records. Unevaluated or incomplete public-database evidence was labeled “Not Reported,”
rather than negative. No nominal p-values, FDR corrections, or probabilistic “mimicry probability” values were
used in computational ranking. Public DOI archiving is intentionally deferred; a submission release would need
to include the frozen source files, input manifests, commands, environment locks, complete predictor outputs,
failed-method artifacts, and source data for every display item.

3 Results
3.1 Initial sequence comparisons across four HLA alleles
A total of 6,400 allele-specific peptide-pair comparisons were evaluated across four target alleles, with 1,600 pairs
per allele. Sequence-similarity metrics were derived across nominally TCR-exposed/core non-anchor positions
P2, P3, P5, P7, and P8. Actual TCR footprints can differ by receptor, allele, register, peptide conformation, and
peptide-flanking-residue contacts. Score distributions were right-skewed across all alleles, with a small subset of
pairs showing elevated nominally TCR-exposed sequence similarity (Supplementary Figure S1). These scores
represent relative BLOSUM62 substitution-matrix resemblance, not percent identity or a probability of cross-
reactivity. The top sequence-ranked pairs within each allele are reported in Supplementary Table S1.
BALF5627-641-TALDO1108-122 scored 0.28125 in HLA-DRB1*13:03 (rank 13/1,600), while BALF5627-641-
TALDO1216-230 scored 0.31429 in HLA-DRB1*15:01 (rank 14/1,600). These are distinct TALDO1 peptide re-
gions. Both had 40.0% identity across the five nominally TCR-exposed positions, with full-core identity of
approximately 22.2% in *13:03 and 33.3% in *15:01.
The shared viral parent peptide was TGGVYHFVKKHVHES. The DRB1*13:03 self peptide was DARLSFD-
KDAMVARA with declared core LSFDKDAMV; the DRB1*15:01 self peptide was SVTKIYNYYKKFSYK
with declared core IYNYYKKFS. The corresponding viral declared cores were YHFVKKHVH and VYH-
FVKKHV, respectively. Per-position score components and continuous presentation-prediction values are
provided in Supplementary Table S5.

3.2 Constrained-null simulation results
Four of the eight audited pairs had nominal unadjusted p values below 0.05, but none met q < 0.05 after
Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment (BH q = 0.0632-0.0819). Because the eight pairs were selected after inspec-
tion of the original screen, these BH values are descriptive post-selection calibration, not confirmatory discovery
statistics. The within-allele maxT values are the more conservative family-wise summary; both BALF5-TALDO1
comparisons had maxT p = 1.0. Complete outputs, including null medians and 95% null intervals, are reported
in Supplementary Table S4. This process tests only conditional sequence-score extremeness and does not provide
information about presentation, TCR recognition, or cross-reactivity.

3.3 Initial Eight-Candidate Evidence Review
Eight candidates selected from the 6,400-pair matrix underwent deep evidence review, yielding two medium-
priority candidates, six holds, and zero high-priority candidates (Supplementary Table S2). The two medium-
priority candidates were BALF5627-641-TALDO1108-122 in HLA-DRB1*13:03 and BALF5627-641-TALDO1216-230
in HLA-DRB1*15:01. Binding-prediction concordance is shown in Supplementary Figure S2.
Both BALF5-TALDO1 pairs showed predicted core-register agreement, although the registers remain un-
confirmed experimentally. Neither pair satisfied full binding consensus. For the HLA-DRB1*13:03 candidate,
the viral arm met the ≤ 5% criterion while the self arm did not. In HLA-DRB1*15:01, neither arm met full-
binding consensus. These thresholds describe the initial review; later predictor outputs differed. No corroborat-
ing experimental evidence under the review protocol, including IEDB eluted-ligand records or PRIDE-sourced
immunopeptidome records, was identified for either self peptide [16]. The six held candidates had register dis-
cordance, insufficient binding support in one or both arms, or absent public experimental evidence. This review
was an initial eight-pair evaluation and remained distinct from the later 49-pair provisional tiers.
3.4 Focused Structural Assessment of Predicted Binding Registers
The two medium-priority pairs were structurally assessed alongside a DRB5*01:01 restriction test. Thirteen
AlphaFold 3 jobs, each returning five models, generated 65 structural models. All passed checks for coordinate
completeness, correct chain identifiers, and peptide-sequence integrity. In HLA-DRB1*13:03, viral parent-nested
median RMSD was 0.445 Å; BALF5 showed consistent coordinate recovery, whereas TALDO1108-122 had lower
confidence and large positional uncertainty across the descriptive 5×5 comparison. This candidate’s modeled
pMHC geometry is unresolved, not merely a minor caveat. In HLA-DRB1*15:01, viral and human RMSD values
were 1.712 Å and 0.888 Å, respectively; viral BALF5 parent models varied, while the TALDO1216-230 11-mer was
comparatively consistent. In HLA-DRB5*01:01, viral RMSD was 0.247 Å and longer parent-peptide modeling
did not stabilize either competing TALDO1 register.
Models of shorter 11-mers frequently converged to consistent placements, but these placements were rarely
retained when parent 15-mers were modeled, producing alternate poses and register shifts (Figure 3). Peptide-
level pLDDT and peptide-chain ipTM were consequently examined separately from global pMHC metrics; HLA
confidence can obscure peptide uncertainty. No discrete pocket-occupancy or core-start classifier was validated
for these AF3 models. Thus, RMSD, pLDDT, and ipTM are descriptive confidence and sensitivity measures, not
register-state measurements; sequence-predictor agreement did not establish atomic-backbone structural confir-
mation.

3.5 Cross-Method Structural Reproducibility
PANDORA coupled with MODELLER was used for cross-method structural reproducibility [17, 18]. The re-
ciprocal PANDORA controls 1BX2 and 6CQQ were separate from the Hy.2E11 structural reference described
above; both passed the pre-defined calibration gate: 120 models were assessed by expected-register recovery,
adjacent-decoy comparison, sequence quality control, and template-leakage checks. Each control had at least
one top-five model with expected-register core-backbone RMSD below 2 Å, lower top-five median RMSD than
adjacent register decoys, and passed exact-sequence and template-leakage tests.
The subsequent ensemble contained 1,200 models spanning BALF5, TALDO1, and eight P6/P7 charge-
substitution variants across two structural templates and three register placements (20 models per condition).
None of the ten predefined peptides supported the expected NetMHCIIpan/MixMHC2pred register across both
templates. A subsequent AlphaFold dataset comprised 150 models from 30 five-model jobs; seven charge-
substitution comparisons had uncertain register placement and one was not evaluable because of seed mismatch.
RMSD cannot on its own distinguish an integer register shift from a deformation within a register or from flanking-
residue flexibility. Accordingly, these results identify structural uncertainty rather than a validated register state
and do not support interpreting charge substitutions as evidence of disrupted binding.

3.6 Expanded Assessment of 49 Candidate Pairs
An enriched 49-pair evaluation panel was recovered from the 6,400-pair ranking; it is not an independent valida-
tion cohort. NetMHCIIpan, MixMHC2pred, and IEDB-recommended binding prediction profiles were completed
for 98 peptide arms. Forty-four pairs had at least two outputs selecting the same declared nine-mer core for both
arms; this is computational output concordance, not experimental register confirmation. Only BALF5627-641-
TALDO1108-122 in HLA-DRB1*13:03 and BALF5627-641-TALDO1216-230 in HLA-DRB1*15:01 met the com-
bined three-output presentation-support/register routing criterion, with all three percentile ranks ≤ 20% for both
arms. This wording does not imply independent algorithms or biological measurements. Threshold sensitivity
showed that this strict result occurred only at the permissive 20% cutoff; neither pair passed at 10% or below.
Under a relaxed two-of-three-output rule, the DRB1*15:01 pair passed at 10%, whereas the DRB1*13:03 pair
first passed at 20%. Allowing a ±1-residue register tolerance added no pair under the strict rule. Complete con-
tinuous predictor values and the full 49-pair threshold grid are reported in Supplementary Table S5 and the local
machine-readable output package. The two-pair result is conditional routing information, not threshold-robust
support for binding or mimicry.
For public-evidence assessment, 98 automated IEDB API queries returned zero retrievable records be-
cause the endpoint was unavailable. The resulting frozen status was not evaluable, so no candidate could be
assigned high priority. Targeted manual IEDB verification later identified a positive qualitative purified-MHC
competitive-radioactivity binding record for the exact viral 15-mer BALF5627-641 in HLA-DRB1*15:01 (IEDB
Assay 1774674; Hansen et al.) [4, 20]. Its sample context and quantitative affinity were not used here, and it
does not confirm TALDO1216-230 binding, stable presentation, or dual-reactive T-cell recognition.
3.7 Secondary Provisional Classifications
A provisional shortlist contained seven candidates: one computational stratum A, two stratum B, and four stratum
C. The remaining 42 candidates were untiered because evidence was insufficient for priority ranking. These
labels are routing strata, not biological-confidence levels, and are distinct from the earlier eight-pair audit. The
full provisional classification table is reported in Supplementary Table S3.
4 Discussion
4.1 Principal Findings
This multi-stage computational screening and evidence-review workflow routed EBV–human peptide pairs
for later experimental validation. Its central findings were two BALF5–TALDO1 pairings, BALF5627-641-
TALDO1216-230 and BALF5627-641-TALDO1108-122; despite sharing parent proteins, they are not the same epitope
pair. The HLA-DRB1*15:01 pairing was assigned to computational stratum A, while the HLA-DRB1*13:03
pairing was assigned to stratum B. Stratum assignment reflects prespecified computational routing and limited
evidence review, not statistical significance of the underlying sequence similarity: neither lead survived
family-wise sequence-null correction. The outcome is a computational shortlist for in vitro peptide-HLA binding
and register mapping, not a claim of functional molecular mimicry in vivo.
Computational stratum A does not mean “confirmed,” validated, or “highest-confidence biological mimic.”
It means that all three selected outputs met the prespecified 20% routing criterion for both arms and that one viral
arm had an exact-sequence, exact-allele direct-binding record.

4.2 Relevance of BALF5-TALDO1
BALF5-TALDO1 did not have the highest raw sequence-similarity score; EBNA1-ANO2 and EBNA3B-MOG
scored higher. Raw sequence alignment was only the first evidence layer. The BALF5-TALDO1 protein pairing
was represented by the only two allele-specific pairs in the 49-pair cohort that met the permissive three-output
presentation-support/register routing criterion. TALDO1 was retained through the predefined self-peptide cura-
tion and sequence-ranking workflow; this study does not infer TALDO1 involvement in MS from its selection.
In HLA-DRB1*15:01, manual recovery of direct-binding evidence for viral BALF5627-641 offered limited exper-
imental grounding [4, 20]; the TALDO1 self arm remains unconfirmed by direct-binding or eluted-ligand assays.
In HLA-DRB1*13:03, both arms met the routing threshold, but no allele-specific public binding record was veri-

4.3 Contribution of Structural Analyses
Agreement among sequence-based register predictors did not guarantee stable structural coordinates. Shorter 11-
mer inputs often converged in the models analyzed, whereas the same placements were often lost or shifted with
the parent 15-mer input. Peptide-local pLDDT and peptide-chain ipTM were therefore more informative than
global pMHC metrics, which can obscure peptide uncertainty through high HLA-chain confidence. AlphaFold
3 and PANDORA outputs showed register drift in the modeled BALF5/TALDO1 charge-substitution inputs. No
component of the structural workflow passed calibration for TCR–pMHC recognition; the retained PANDORA
calibration is limited to forced-template pMHC register recovery. These analyses limit register-specific inter-
pretation; they do not identify whether the observed uncertainty is a property of modeling conditions or peptide
biophysics. The retained EBNA1-MBP PANDORA register supports computational geometric plausibility, not
native binding.
4.4 Linkage to Prior Research
Bjornevik et al. established the epidemiological link between EBV infection and MS, and Lanz et al. reported
antibody cross-reactivity between EBNA1 and GlialCAM [1, 3]. These mechanisms differ from pMHC class II
T-cell recognition; operationally, T-cell cross-reactivity requires one T cell to recognize more than one distinct
peptide-MHC ligand [22]. Here, claims remain limited to computational candidate prioritization and predicted
pMHC similarity. Literature-associated protein pairs, including EBNA1-ANO2 and EBNA1-MBP, emerged in
the screen, but shared parent proteins must be distinguished from exact peptide epitopes [19]. Literature-informed
pairings were not treated as independent computational validation because their disease association contributed
to protein selection.
4.5 Additional Exploratory Methods
Electrostatic surface-potential comparison was excluded from primary ranking because 15 of 30 required control
outputs failed and 12 were missing; among the three usable outputs, the calibration control did not discriminate
from background decoys. TCRModel2 calibration produced acceptable models for 1ZGL but incorrect models for
1YMM and 2WBJ under DockQ and CAPRI criteria. These failures prevent candidate-specific TCR-recognition
claims; they do not provide biological evidence that cross-reactivity is absent in vivo.

4.6 Limitations
The search space was limited to pre-curated viral epitopes, selected myelin/CNS proteins, and four HLA-DRB1
alleles; HLA-DQ and HLA-DP loci, broader haplotype effects, and proteome-wide background sampling were
omitted. Canonical P1/P4/P6/P9 positions were used as a positional convention, not an assertion that anchor chem-
istry is uniform across alleles or peptide contexts. Rankings shifted under nominally TCR-exposed BLOSUM62,
full-core BLOSUM62, and Grantham-distance formulations. The five-position score is a rational heuristic but
lacks a benchmark with verified cross-reactive and specificity-negative TCR systems. Predictors may share train-
ing data, limiting the independence of their agreement. The unavailable public-evidence endpoint and absent self-
peptide records did not establish biological absence of presentation. In silico modeling also cannot account for
endogenous antigen processing, HLA-DM-mediated peptide editing, pMHC stability, peptide-flanking-residue
effects, antigen-presenting-cell context, or downstream TCR-signaling thresholds [23].
4.7 Experimental Next Steps
Peptide-HLA binding can be quantified for viral and self peptides against recombinant HLA-DRB1 molecules by
fluorescence polarization or surface plasmon resonance. Register mapping may use alanine-scanning mutagenesis
and structural methods such as X-ray crystallography or cryo-EM. Matched T cells should be tested against both
peptide-HLA complexes for dual functional activation. Endogenous processing can be assessed through peptide
elution and mass spectrometry from infected or inflamed antigen-presenting cells.
5 Conclusions
The first experimental-routing candidate is HLA-DRB1*15:01 BALF5627-641-TALDO1216-230, because it met the
permissive 20% three-output routing criterion and has exact allele-specific viral-arm IEDB binding evidence
[4, 20]. This priority does not imply significant sequence similarity, strong predicted affinity, stable register
occupancy, or mimicry. Recombinant peptide-HLA binding and kinetic-stability assays should test viral and self
arms separately, followed by register mapping, HLA-DM susceptibility testing, ligand-elution mass spectrometry
in relevant antigen-presenting cells, and matched-TCR dual-recognition assays. The HLA-DRB1*13:03 BALF5–
TALDO1 pair is a conditional secondary routing candidate: both arms met the same permissive threshold, but
its modeled geometry is unresolved and no allele-specific public record was verified. Natural presentation and
cross-reactivity remain untested for both candidates.
